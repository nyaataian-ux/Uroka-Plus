01
Project Overview

Two molecules.
One capsule.
Better outcomes.

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia affects more than 50% of men over 50 and up to 90% by the age of 80. In clinical practice, the challenge isn't diagnosing BPH — it's convincing the prescriber to start the right therapy early enough to prevent disease progression, acute urinary retention, and surgery.

Uroka Plus is Mega We Care's fixed-dose combination of Dutasteride 0.5mg (a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor that shrinks the prostate) and Tamsulosin 0.4mg (an alpha-1A adrenergic blocker that immediately improves urine flow). Together, they deliver a dual mechanism that no monotherapy can match.

The visual aid campaign was built around a single strategic idea: "Start right." The earlier combination therapy begins, the greater the risk reduction — from AUR to BPH-related surgery. Every slide reinforces this urgency with clinical evidence and intuitive illustration.

03
Visual Aid Slides — Annotated

12 slides.
One argument.

Slide 01
Campaign Opener · Conceptual Hook
The Right Start — A Kinked Tap Metaphor

The campaign opens not with the product, but with a problem visualised. A chrome tap with a knot in its pipe — a single drop falling — is an instantly legible metaphor for obstructed urine flow. The visual makes the patient's discomfort tangible before a molecule is introduced. The CombAT study citation grounds the opening in clinical evidence immediately.

Slide 02
Product Introduction
Uroka Plus — Dual Control for Improved QOL

The product reveal slide introduces both molecules side-by-side with bold typographic hierarchy — DUTASTERIDE in red, TAMSULOSIN in navy — along with their mechanism classes and clinical outcomes. The "Delivers World Class European Quality" badge in the top-right corner signals manufacturing pedigree without requiring a dedicated slide.

Slide 03
Formulation Technology
The Two-in-One Capsule — Engineering the Dual Release

A photographed cross-section of the capsule reveals the engineering inside: a Dutasteride softgel capsule surrounded by Tamsulosin pellets, all within a hard outer shell. Three technical advantage tiles (gastroprotective layer, sub-1.4mm pellet size, soft-gel Dutasteride isolation) explain why this formulation outperforms dual-pill regimens on stability, side effects, and compliance.

Slide 04 · Dutasteride MOA
Dutasteride — Dual 5AR Inhibition Mechanism

The Dutasteride MOA diagram shows testosterone being blocked at both Type 1 and Type 2 5-alpha reductase receptors simultaneously — preventing DHT conversion and resulting in prostate volume reduction. The key "Ideal Choice" benefits and clinical indications for enlarged prostate are listed alongside the anatomical pathway illustration.

Slide 05 · Tamsulosin MOA
Tamsulosin — Uroselective α1A Blockade

The bladder anatomy illustration demonstrates Tamsulosin's mechanism in two stages: tension in the constricted urethra above, then relaxation and improved flow below. Six clinical advantages — from rapid 1-week onset to 6-year safety data — are listed to address the prescriber's key concerns about alpha-blockers.

Slide 06
Combination Benefits
Multiple Benefits of Full Dose Combination

The synergy slide brings both MOA diagrams together, side-by-side — Dutasteride shrinking the prostate, Tamsulosin relaxing the muscle — with the combined clinical benefits of Uroka Plus listed: fast response under 2 weeks, 28% prostate size reduction, AUR risk reduction, 48% IPSS improvement, and Qmax increases of 1.2–4.0 ml/s. This is the "why both" argument delivered visually.

Slide 07
Clinical Decision Tool
BPH Treatment Algorithm — IPSS-Based Decision Tree

A clean hexagonal decision tree takes the prescriber from "BPH Patient" through IPSS scoring (≤7 vs >7), bothersome symptom severity, and prostate volume/PSA thresholds — to the appropriate therapy class. Where prostate ≥30ml and PSA ≥1.5ng/ml: 5-ARI, alpha blocker, or combination. This slide is designed to be used live in consultation as a shared decision-making tool.

Slide 08 · Early CT Evidence
Start Before 6 Months — AUR Risk & IPSS Data

The early combination therapy evidence slide is the campaign's strongest prescribing argument: starting CT before 6 months reduces AUR/S risk 3-fold vs delayed CT, achieves ≥50% IPSS improvement in 60.8% of patients vs 50.5%, and delivers significantly higher responder rates. Source: World Journal of Urology (2021). The product box is prominent — this is the prescribing close.

Slide 09 · Early vs Late CT
Early Treatment Benefits vs Delay Increases Risks

A character-driven comparison card — an animated illustration of a confident patient (early CT: 3× risk reduction) vs a worried patient (late CT: higher event incidence, symptom deterioration). The contrast is simple and emotionally direct, making the early treatment argument feel personally relevant to the doctor's patient sitting across the desk.

Slide 10
Competitive Comparison
Synergy Table — Uroka Plus vs 5ARIs vs α-Blockers

A structured comparative table sets Uroka Plus against monotherapy options across six clinical outcomes: prostate volume reduction, PV maintenance, symptom/flow improvement, speed of onset, halting progression, and AUR/surgery risk reduction. Uroka Plus is the only therapy to check all six boxes — the table is the visual argument for combination therapy superiority, laid out without bias.

Slide 11
Clinical Evidence · CombAT Study
CombAT Study — 4-Year IPSS Data: Total, Storage & Voiding

The CombAT study bar chart (BJU Int. 2011;107(9):1426–31) is the campaign's crown jewel evidence slide. Three grouped bar charts compare combination therapy, dutasteride alone, and tamsulosin alone across Total IPSS (−6.3 vs −5.3 vs −3.8), Storage sub-score (−2.3 vs −1.9 vs −1.4), and Voiding sub-score (−4.0 vs −3.5 vs −2.4). Combination wins every measure — the chart does the talking.

Slide 12
Prescribing Close
Indications, Dosage & Patient-Facing Close

The campaign closes with an African doctor-patient image — two men, one younger and confident, one older and relieved — communicating quality of life restoration. The RX indications (moderate to severe BPH, AUR risk reduction) and dosage (one capsule daily) are stated plainly. The close is designed to leave the prescriber with a positive, human image and a clear action.

04
Collateral · Clinic Touch Point

The Tent Card
extends the campaign.

Clinic Tent Card · Point of Care

Prostate anatomy at the doctor's desk

The Uroka Plus tent card is designed to sit permanently on the urologist's or GP's desk — a constant, passive communication tool. It features a detailed anatomical cross-section of the prostate and bladder (Transition Zone, Peripheral Zone, AFS, Urethra, Seminal Vesicle, Ejaculatory Duct), giving the physician a reference point during consultation while keeping the Uroka Plus branding in the doctor's line of sight.

The tent format was chosen specifically for desk placement — it stands independently, faces the doctor, and doubles as patient-education material when turned to face the patient.

05
Design Rationale

Why each
choice was made.

The Knotted Tap Opener

A chrome tap with a tied knot — obstructed flow as an engineering metaphor. It's immediately understood by anyone who's turned a tap, making the patient's condition tangible before a word of pharmacology is introduced. Physicians respond to visual metaphors that their patients would understand.

Red + Sky Blue Identity Split

Dutasteride is red — bold, active, structural — because it physically changes the prostate. Tamsulosin is sky blue — fluid, flowing, immediate — because it clears the urinary path. The colour coding makes the dual-mechanism argument instinctively memorable without explanation.

The Treatment Algorithm Slide

Designing a decision tree as a detailing tool rather than a reference document was a deliberate choice. The hexagonal node style invites the doctor to trace the patient's pathway with a finger, making the slide interactive rather than passive — and positioning Uroka Plus at the end of the clinical logic, not inserted into it.

CombAT Data Visualisation

The 4-year CombAT study data could have been presented as a table. Instead, a three-grouped bar chart makes the combination therapy superiority across Total, Storage, and Voiding sub-scores immediately visible. The orange bars (combination) consistently extend further — the eye processes the advantage in milliseconds.

Early vs Late — Character Illustrations

Two illustrated patient avatars — confident and worried — reduce a complex clinical risk analysis to an emotional comparison. This slide was designed for situations where a busy GP needs a human reason to change prescribing behaviour, not another data table. Emotion supports evidence; it doesn't replace it.

African Representation in the Close

The prescribing close features two African men — a doctor and a patient — both smiling. In a urology campaign, closing on QOL restoration rather than clinical data leaves the prescriber with a positive emotional frame. The doctor sees a patient outcome, not a pharmacological endpoint.
